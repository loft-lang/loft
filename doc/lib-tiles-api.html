pub fn draw(surface: integer, sheet: integer, tile: integer, x: integer, y: integer, flip_h: boolean = false, flip_v: boolean = false, flip_d: boolean = false, shade: integer = 0)

Draw tile tile of sheet with its top-left at (x, y), in any of the eight orientations. A sprite is a tile drawn this way. Transparent pixels and whatever falls off the surface are skipped. shade darkens it: 0 is the tile as it is, and each shade up to SHADES - 1 is darker, down to about a quarter of the light, so the darkest still shows what is there. A shade costs nothing: each sheet keeps its palette at every shade.

pub fn draw_turned(surface: integer, sheet: integer, tile: integer, x: integer, y: integer, degrees: integer, pivot: (integer, integer), flip_h: boolean = false, flip_v: boolean = false, flip_d: boolean = false, shade: integer = 0)

Draw tile tile of sheet turned degrees clockwise about its pixel pivot (x, y within the tile): placed as draw places it at (x, y), oriented by the flips first, then turned — a character's slight lean as it walks, a weapon held at an angle or swung. Every pixel of the tile lands on exactly one pixel of the surface (the turn is whole quarter turns and three shears of whole rows and columns), so the pixel art keeps its pixels and colours at any angle; 0 degrees is draw, and 90, 180 and 270 are its quarter turns. The shade, transparency and clipping are draw's.

pub fn draw_ground(surface: integer, sheet: integer, tile: integer, x: integer, y: integer, lift: (integer, integer, integer, integer), shade: (integer, integer, integer, integer) = (0, 0, 0, 0), flip_h: boolean = false, flip_v: boolean = false, flip_d: boolean = false)

Draw tile tile of sheet as a piece of ground that slopes and is lit gradually: its place is the plain draw's at (x, y), with its corners — top-left, top-right, bottom-left, bottom-right — lifted lift pixels (up is positive, so (4, 4, 0, 0) leans its far edge up a step) and darkened shade sixteenths of a shade (SHADE_PARTS is one whole shade darker; past SHADES - 1 whole shades it stays the darkest).

Each column of the tile stays a column of pixels, stretched or squeezed between its top and bottom edges, whose lifts are interpolated from the corners at the column's centre. So two tiles that give their shared corners the same lifts meet exactly, with no gap — a slope, a mound, a ramp onto a higher floor; and a drop between them shows as the gap a wall tile (or a darker one) fills, drawn the same way. The darkness is interpolated across the tile and shared between the two nearest shades by a fixed 4x4 dither, so light fades smoothly within a tile and the pattern stays with the ground as the view moves. Over a plain draw it costs a little per column and a table lookup per pixel; a tile with every lift and shade 0 draws exactly what draw draws. Orientation, transparency and clipping are draw's.
